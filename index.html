How the cost model was derived

The rulebook has no unit-construction rules — point values appear only as fixed numbers printed on each card. This model is a least-squares fit to those ten published costs, so it is a reconstruction of the designer's pricing, not an official rule.

The formula

points = 1.205 x ATK(sum of bands)
     + 2.047 x DEF(green)
     + 6.714 x Structure
     + 0.163 x (weapon attack dice, rockets counted x4)
     + 0.373 x (attack dice on energy weapons)
     + 1.888 x (total weapon range in inches)
     + 2.411 x Hovering
     + 2.500 x specialty armor points
then round to the nearest 5.

What this model can and cannot tell you

Ten cards, seven free parameters. The fit is tight, but it is not unique — other formulas also reproduce all ten costs. Treat the numbers as a consistent pricing scheme rather than the designer's actual arithmetic.

Armor and Structure cannot be separated. Across the ten units they correlate at 0.96 (they are equal on six of ten cards), so the fit cannot tell what Armor costs on its own. Structure carries the whole durability term here; if you build a unit with unusually lopsided Armor, expect the model to misprice it.

Fuel, Max Heat and Movement came out near zero and are excluded. That is a limitation of the sample, not a claim they are free — Dusk Reaper carries +2 Fuel over Storm Hulk and the model absorbs that elsewhere.

Specialty armor is the one non-fitted term. Rulebook section 9.0 prices ablative and reactive armor at 5 points for 2, so that rate is fixed at 2.5 per point rather than fitted.

Weapon cost is carried almost entirely by range. For Storm Hulk the range term is 75 of its 150 points while the attack-dice term is 4. The fit put the weight there because range varies more across the ten cards than dice do; a real construction system would probably split that differently, so exotic loadouts (a very long-ranged weak gun, or a short-ranged brutal one) are where this model is least trustworthy.

Expected error on a new unit is around 3 points, based on leave-one-out cross-validation (worst case 7). For units close in shape to the official ten, it will be tighter.
